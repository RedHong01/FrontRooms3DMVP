**What this means for the furniture.** In 1990 a room is never furnished only with 1990 products. A furniture store, hotel liquidator or second-hand office in 1990 would hold:
- (a) **current stock**, c. 1985-1996;
- (b) **recent stock**: 1975-85 pieces that were still in use, or still in the catalogue under the same model number;
- (c) **timeless types** such as pallets, crates, bentwood coat stands and ladder-backs, which were made the same way for decades.

The bar for a modelled anachronism is therefore narrow. It is a **feature that could not exist, or would look wrong, in a c. 1990 room**: post-1996 technology, or post-2000 styling. An old style is not an anachronism. This report uses four labels:
- **core-era**: the type, finish and colour peaked in 1985-96.
- **older-plausible**: the type peaked in 1965-85 and is natural second-hand, hotel or estate stock in 1990.
- **timeless**: the type's form was unchanged over the whole period.
- **anachronistic**: it contradicts the setting.
