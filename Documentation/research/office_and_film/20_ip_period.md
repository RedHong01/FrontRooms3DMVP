## 1. The founding photo (2002, Oshkosh)

**What and where.** The photo was taken on 12 June 2002 at 807 Oregon Street, Oshkosh, Wisconsin, on the second storey of the former Rohner's Home Furnishings building, while the new tenant (a HobbyTown USA branch) was renovating it. It was shot on a Sony Cyber-shot, the file is "Dsc00161.jpg", and it was posted to the HobbyTown Oshkosh site on 2 March 2003 ([Wikipedia, The Backrooms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Backrooms); [PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2026/06/30/how-backrooms-all-started-with-this-photograph/) confirms the camera and file name). Rohner's occupied 807 and 811 Oregon Street "for much of the 20th century", and the HobbyTown blog noted water damage that required the area to be cleared (Wikipedia, above).

**Building history.**
- The building predates 1913: the first advertisement for the department store that first occupied it dates from 1913, and it was about 110 years old in 2024 ([WFRV / wearegreenbay](https://digital-release.wearegreenbay.com/?p=1710929)). PBS Wisconsin calls it a major 1920s department store ([PBS Wisconsin](https://pbswisconsin.org/article/how-a-furniture-store-in-oshkosh-became-a-portal-to-another-dimension/)). The two sources disagree on the decade; the 1913 advertisement is the more specific evidence.
- It then became the Rohner's furniture showroom. HobbyTown owner Bob Mazza (store opened 1994) bought the building in 2003 (WFRV, above). PBS describes the upstairs as "very old, water damaged" (PBS Wisconsin, above).
- **Date conflict:** PBS says the photo was taken during a 2003 renovation and first reached 4chan in 2011. Wikipedia gives 12 June 2002 (camera date) and 2019 for the Backrooms post. Waxy.org's headline also says "a 2003 photo" ([waxy.org](https://waxy.org/2024/05/internet-sleuths-find-origin-of-the-photo-that-inspired-the-backrooms)). The likeliest reading: shot in 2002, published on the HobbyTown site in 2003.
- **Rohner's closing year: NOT FOUND.** No fetched source dates the end of the furniture store. UNVERIFIED.

**Reading.** The founding image is a **2002 photograph of a 1980s–90s furniture-store fit-out** (beige chevron vinyl paper, beige broadloom, drop ceiling with fluorescent troffers) inside a building from before 1913. Nothing in it is from 2002 itself; it was already dated, water-damaged and about to be demolished. That is why the IP keeps reaching back to "the late 80s/early 90s" even though the photo was taken in 2002.

## 2. The 2019 4chan origin and the creepypasta's implied era

- First posted 12–13 May 2019 on 4chan's /x/ (paranormal) board, in a thread asking for disquieting images that feel "off" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Backrooms)).
- The reply that named it describes the stink of old moist carpet, "the madness of mono-yellow", and fluorescent lights at maximum hum-buzz (Wikipedia, above). It names **no year**. Its period cues are sensory: carpet, mono-yellow wallpaper and fluorescent hum.
- The reply's key verb is "noclip", a PC-game cheat term (see §5.3 for its 1990s origin). So the creepypasta's frame of reference is the 1990s–2000s first-person shooter: you fall *out of the level geometry*. This is a 1990s cultural cue, not a statement about when the room exists.
- Wikipedia's later framing that the Backrooms is "a poorly remembered recollection of the late 1990s and early 2000s" belongs to Kane Parsons' series, via a 29 March 2022 Vice article, not to the 2019 creepypasta ([Wikipedia, The Backrooms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Backrooms); [Wikipedia, web series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backrooms_(web_series))). The search summary attributes the "cloudy recollection" wording to Vice writer Andrew Lloyd, with the family-photo line being Kane's own words (UNVERIFIED: Vice not fetched).

**Reading.** The 2019 origin is *undated*. It inherits its period from the photo (late-20th-century furniture-store back room) and from 1990s game language (noclip).

- **Conflict:** Dazed says the first short is "set in 1995" ([Dazed](https://dazeddigital.com/film-tv/article/62125/1/a24-youngest-ever-director-kane-parsons-the-backrooms-creepypasta-into-cinema)). Two explainers give 4 July 1991, and Wikipedia gives 1996 for the recovery. Dazed probably merged the ep. 1 and Found Footage #2 dates. Use **1991**.
- Async's **founding year is never stated**. Wikipedia says the series is "set primarily in the early 1990s" and Async discovered the Backrooms in the late 1980s ([Wikipedia, web series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backrooms_(web_series))); Ghost in My Machine lists the founding year as an open question.

**Visual period of the series.**
- Found-footage VHS / camcorder look; Async material reads as late-80s institutional video and documents ([Taylor Holmes](https://taylorholmes.com/2026/05/29/explaining-the-backrooms-youtube-videos-prior-to-watching-the-movie/)).
- Kane (b. 18 June 2005, so 16 at launch: [Wikipedia, Kane Parsons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kane_Parsons)) builds the look from family photos where "the white balance is all off" (Vice 2022, quoted on [Wikipedia, web series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backrooms_(web_series))). His references are late-90s/early-2000s photos, which **predate his own memory**. The fiction is pinned to 1988–1991, but the *picture quality* is borrowed from c. 1997–2004 consumer flash photography.
- The props on screen are real-history objects with factory dates: a 1973 troffer, a 1975 ballast and Sylvania T12 tubes (above). So the series' rule is **"objects are older than the date on the tape."** A 1989 survey finds 1973 hardware.

**Reading.** Kane's canon is **1988–1991** (Async front world), with prehistory back to 1972/1982 and a tail to 1995/96. Its texture is **late 1990s / early 2000s** photographic memory.
