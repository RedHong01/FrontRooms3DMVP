## 0. TL;DR for the Office kit

1. **Canon office = emptiness with dispensers and dead windows.** wikidot Level 4 is an empty office building with water coolers, vending machines, fountains and blacked-out windows; Fandom Level 4 adds barren rooms + cubicles, carpet indents where furniture once stood, many dead fluorescents, and furniture only in *smaller* rooms. Red's target (islands + appliances on an empty floor) sits comfortably inside this canon.
2. **Period = late 1980s-mid 1990s** (Fandom Level 153: peeling dull wallpaper, antiquated drop ceilings, yellowing lights, worn dusty carpet, antiquated terminals). No flat screens/LEDs/laptops.
3. **Canon furniture wrongness = stretch, sink/fuse, repeat, mound** (Kane: Found Footage #2 elongated furniture; Static Dead End furniture phased into walls/floor + carpet hill with chair; Everything Must Go repeating signs phasing into floor). The film adds **piles** of intact 90s furniture-store stock. Nothing is ever broken.
4. **The IP's founding image is an emptied furniture store** (Rohner's Home Furnishings, Oshkosh; photographed 2002 with a Sony Cyber-shot — not a Nikon, fix VISUAL_RESEARCH_LOOKDEV.md). So domestic furniture piles are thematically native, office furniture comes from the wiki/games.
5. **Games:** the big co-op games furnish offices with a small countable catalogue (desks, chairs, book stacks, water dispensers, vending machines) and lean on fog/lighting; the convincing small-team liminal games (POOLS and Anemoiapolis are **Unity**; Exit 8 UE5) win with restraint, real dimensions and photo-referenced materials.
6. **Layout:** 0.61 m module everywhere (3 m cell = 5 modules); islands of 4-6 stations (1.8-2.4 m each), panels 1.37/1.65 m; real aisles 0.9-1.2 m, gameplay aisle 2-2.5 m on the door axis; appliances against walls; 1-2 dark windows per room.
7. **Lighting:** real grid 2x4 troffers at 8'x10' (~38 fc), but light only 30-60%; reflectances 0.8/0.5/0.2; haze; off-white-balance cool grade.
8. **Licensing:** wiki text/images are CC BY-SA 3.0 (games using them must be CC BY-SA 3.0 or GPLv3); concepts are free to use. Kane's series and the film are all-rights-reserved. Use concepts only, own/CC0 assets, and add a courtesy credit.

### 1.3 Distorted / fused / piled furniture in the series

Verified canon precedents (all before or alongside the film):
1. **Found Footage #2 (2022-08-21):** the protagonist finds "uneven architecture, elongated furniture, and large open spaces in states of disrepair" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backrooms_(web_series) . Distortion type = **non-uniform scale (stretch)** of normal furniture.
2. **Static Dead End (2025-02-12):** "stretched wallpaper, irregular floor depth, and furniture phased into the walls" (Wikipedia, above). Recap: wallpaper stretched horizontally "like a broken UV texture map"; objects half-fused into floor and walls; a carpet hill with a single plastic chair on top — https://taylorholmes.com/2026/06/02/explaining-the-backrooms-youtube-episodes-18-22/ . Distortion types = **interpenetration with architecture, UV stretching, terrain deformation (carpet hill)**.
3. **Everything Must Go (2026-08-18, 1990-06-18 Async record):** a sequence of hanging "EVERYTHING MUST GO!" signs **repeating and phasing into the floor** (Wikipedia, above). Extended-cut recap: wood-panelled furniture-storage space with coat racks, lawn furniture, a mannequin by a ship's wheel, a wheel half-submerged in the floor, a TV flashing blue, objects protruding from walls and floors — https://www.netflixjunkie.com/hollywood-news-backrooms-everything-must-go-a-deep-dive-into-the-extended-cut-of-kane-parsons-horror-blockbuster/ . Distortion types = **array repetition (copy-paste along a line), half-submersion**. Made in Blender on a laptop in about two weeks — https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260821-backrooms-everything-must-go/ (prior-run fetch, not re-fetched: treat as lead)

=> Canon "furniture wrongness" vocabulary = **stretch, repeat, sink, fuse, mound**. Nothing is smashed or burnt; objects stay intact and recognisable. This matches the film stills Red described (pieces duplicated and pasted, interpenetrating, unbroken).

## 2. Backrooms Wiki (wikidot) and Fandom — office-like levels

### 2.1 Level 4 "Abandoned Office" (wikidot)

Verified 2026-10-02 at https://backrooms-wiki.wikidot.com/level-4 (author credit u/M654zy from Reddit; last edited 29 May 2025; CC BY-SA 3.0):
- It "resembles an empty office building" and is almost completely devoid of furniture.
- Rooms, some with windows; most windows are blacked out; un-blacked windows are framed as a hazard.
- Scattered **water coolers, vending machines and fountains** dispensing Almond Water; exits are office-style stairwells and occasional elevators.
- The page does **not** describe lighting, carpet, ceiling, cubicles, desks, chairs or computers.
- Header image: "Empty office" by Ged Carroll, **CC BY 2.0**, Flickr https://www.flickr.com/photos/renaissancechambara/6141729818 (licence box on the Level 4 page). This is a real photograph of an empty office with windows; it is CC BY 2.0, so it could legally be used as a *reference* with attribution, but we should still not ship it.

Implication: in wikidot canon the office is defined by **absence** (empty open floor + a few dispensers + dead windows), not by cubicle clutter. Red's target image (cubicle islands, copier, vending, cooler, dark interior window) is therefore a *richer* office than wikidot Level 4 — closer to the Fandom sub-levels and to the games.

### 2.2 Fandom Level 4 and sub-levels (4.3 "The Cubicles", Level 153 "Antique Workspace")

The Fandom wiki (backrooms.fandom.com) is a *separate* community canon from wikidot; its pages are also CC BY-SA ("Community content is available under CC-BY-SA unless otherwise noted" footer). WebFetch returns HTTP 402 for Fandom, so these were read in the built-in browser on 2026-10-02.

**Fandom Level 4 "The Abandoned Office"** — https://backrooms.fandom.com/wiki/Level_4 (author "Jamie" per author box; Class Habitable):
- "a sprawling complex of barren rooms and cubicles", resembling a modern commercial office. Most rooms featureless and empty.
- **Carpet indents** where furniture used to stand ("indents throughout the carpeting suggest that furniture may have been present") — a great decal idea: furniture *ghosts* on empty floor.
- **Many fluorescent lights do not function**, and their wall switches are missing.
- Vending machines strewn about; multiple stairwells that loop back to the same floor; emergency exits, elevators, trapdoors.
- Windows look onto a fake, static sky in constant heavy rain and thick fog (the page has a "show rain" toggle); the rain pattering on windows is the main ambience, plus pipe rumble; temperature 12-18 °C.
- **Two-tier furnishing rule:** the large open areas are bereft of large furniture; the *smaller* rooms (workrooms, hallways, confined offices) contain desks and "desktop computers of questionable quality" and partly working fluorescents.
- Image captions on page: "A typical workroom, lined with desks"; "A smaller, empty office. The lights are switched on, an aspect not shared with the level's larger rooms." (lit small rooms vs. dark big rooms).

**Level 4.3 "The Cubicles"** — https://backrooms.fandom.com/wiki/Level_4.3 :
- "an endless office filled with cubicles", hallways connecting areas; cubicles contain black office chairs, pencils, sharpeners, erasers, tables, paper, computers (not all functional); food/drink in refrigerators.
- Clusters of **load-bearing walls grouped closely together** (column/wall clusters as navigation landmarks).
- Permanent **white fog**; unnaturally humid -> **mould, lichen, moss** on cubicles, ceilings, floors, walls; further in = more decrepit, cubicles destroyed and overgrown.
- Sub-rooms: meeting rooms, lavatories, managers' offices; laptops lying around.
- Ambience: paper shuffling, pencils writing, muffled voices, muffled rain from Level 4.

**Level 153 "Antique Workspace"** — https://backrooms.fandom.com/wiki/Level_153 :
- Indoor office building, **no windows**; architecture of the **late 1980s to mid-1990s**.
- **Peeling dull-coloured wallpaper** with period wall decor, **antiquated drop ceilings** with **yellowing ceiling lights**, **worn, dusty carpeting**.
- Workstations = office chair + work desk + office supplies + **antiquated computer terminals** (moderately good condition, prone to file corruption).
- Unreliable wiring: outages per sector, **exposed wires**, many **fire extinguishers and sprinklers**.
- **Break Rooms**: wood-panelled walls, unusually dim lighting; vending machines, **water coolers, coffee machines**, food cabinets, large freezers.
- Libraries ("Library of Time") with bookshelves.

"Cubicle Hell" (sub-level of The Corrupted Instability, Fandom): office strewn with clothes, worksheets, items and **scattered cubicles**, with a computer from roughly early-1970s to 1990s — UNVERIFIED (search snippet only, https://backrooms.fandom.com/wiki/The_Corrupted_Instability).

## 3. The original 2019 image and the Oshkosh furniture store

Verified (Wikipedia https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Backrooms, fetched 2026-10-02):
- First Backrooms post: 2019-05-12 on 4chan /x/, in a thread asking for "disquieting images that just feel 'off'".
- Photo shows beige carpet, yellow wallpaper with a repeating pattern, a drop ceiling with rectangular light fixtures, interior partition walls — **no doors, windows or furniture**.
- Taken 2002-06-12 at 807 Oregon Street, Oshkosh, Wisconsin — the former **Rohner's Home Furnishings** building — during renovation of the second storey for a **HobbyTown USA** branch; camera **Sony Cyber-shot**, file "Dsc00161.jpg", posted on the HobbyTown site 2003-03-02 captioned "East (Oval) room". Found in May 2024 by a Backrooms Discord via the Wayback Machine. The space became an RC racing track; 2024 roof disrepair and crowdfunding; building sold 2025.
- PBS Wisconsin adds: the building began as a major 1920s department store; the photo shows the **water-damaged upstairs**; owner Bob Mazza converted it to a hobby shop + indoor RC track — https://pbswisconsin.org/article/how-a-furniture-store-in-oshkosh-became-a-portal-to-another-dimension/
- Galerie (film production designer Danny Vermette): the photo is just badly white-balanced; the real paper is a **beige chevron** by 1990s brand Borden Home Wallcoverings — https://galeriemagazine.com/how-horror-hit-backrooms-terrorizes-viewers-with-design/

Correction for the project docs: `Documentation/VISUAL_RESEARCH_LOOKDEV.md` says the photo was a "Nikon Coolpix" shot; Wikipedia and PetaPixel (https://petapixel.com/2026/06/30/how-backrooms-all-started-with-this-photograph/) both say **Sony Cyber-shot**. Fix that line.

Why it matters for the Office: the *founding* image of the IP is the **back storage/upper floor of a furniture store, emptied of furniture**. The film turns this into Clark's furniture showroom; Everything Must Go turns it into a furniture-storage Backrooms room. So "piles of furniture-store stock" are thematically native to the IP — they are the furniture that *should have been* in the empty room. Office furniture (cubicles/CRTs) is a Wiki/games addition, not from the photo.

Observations (synthesis):
- The two big co-op games treat the office as **sparse rooms + a few countable furniture types + cameras/windows hazards**. Furniture is a small catalogue reused heavily; the vibe is carried by lighting and fog (Push Square).

## 5. Licensing (CC BY-SA 3.0) — what a student game may use

Verified 2026-10-02 at https://backrooms-wiki.wikidot.com/licensing-guide :
- All wiki text is CC BY-SA 3.0; once published it cannot be revoked. Attribution = creator name + licence version + source link.
- Images are licensed individually in each page's licence box (filename, author, licence, source). CC BY-SA pages may embed CC BY images but not NC/ND ones. Cropping/colour edits are "modifications" (note them); adding content makes a "derivative" that must be licensed itself.
- **Ideas vs. text:** concepts from the wiki may be built upon and remixed; copying text with minor changes is infringement; unfinished drafts need permission.
- **Games:** a game built on wiki content must be released under CC BY-SA 3.0 or GPLv3, with prominent attribution, no DRM if paid, no EULA restrictions.
- Summary phrase from the guide: credit is required and "whatever you make must be shared under the same license".

What this means for FrontRooms (practical reading, not legal advice):
1. **Safe:** using generic concepts — "an endless empty office", "blacked-out windows", "water coolers and vending machines". These are unprotectable ideas and also common to real offices. Naming a zone "Office" or "Level 4-inspired" is fine.
2. **Triggers share-alike:** pasting wiki text (level descriptions, entity text) into in-game notes, signage, or UI; using wiki images/textures; using wiki-specific named inventions (e.g. "Almond Water", named entities) *as written*. If FrontRooms ships any of that, the game (or at least that content) must go out under CC BY-SA 3.0 / GPLv3 with credits.
3. **Separate owners:** Kane Pixels' series/Async branding and the A24 film are NOT CC-licensed — use for study only, never assets, names, logos or stills. (See wiki page for Kane Pixels being separate IP: UNVERIFIED on the licensing guide; Kane's work was never published to the wiki.)
4. Recommendation: keep FrontRooms on the "concept only" side — own models, own textures (CC0 sources), own text — and add a credits line "Inspired by the Backrooms creepypasta and the Backrooms Wiki community (CC BY-SA 3.0)" as courtesy.

## 7. Layout rules

Real-world numbers (verified where URL given) and the FrontRooms translation. Units: 1 ft = 0.3048 m; the 2-ft module = 0.61 m.

**R1. One module for everything.** Ceiling grid, carpet tiles and cubicle panels in real offices share the 2-ft (0.61 m) module (T shows 2'x2' tiles; troffers are 2'x4'). FrontRooms' 3 m maze cell is almost exactly 10 ft (3.05 m) = 5 modules, so snap ceiling-grid origin, carpet-tile origin, column centres and island origins to 0.6 m inside each cell; this alone removes the "props placed on a float grid" look. (Module claim: standard trade sizes, UNVERIFIED source this run.)

**R2. Two furnishing tiers (canon).** Big open areas are almost empty; small rooms carry the furniture — Fandom Level 4 says the large open areas are "bereft of large furniture and appliances" while smaller workrooms hold desks and computers (https://backrooms.fandom.com/wiki/Level_4); wikidot Level 4 is "almost completely devoid of furniture" (https://backrooms-wiki.wikidot.com/level-4). Suggested seed mix for `FrontRoomsOfficeKit.Dress`: ~45% *open hall* (columns + 0-1 island + dispensers on walls + carpet ghosts), ~40% *workroom* (2-4 islands), ~15% *special* (break room, copier/filing room, manager office with interior window, or anomaly/pile room).

**R3. Cubicle islands.** Workstation footprints 6'x6' (1.83 m), 6'x8' or 8'x8' (2.44 m) (https://www.officefurniture2go.com/answers/cubicles-workstations/how-do-i-plan-a-cubicle-layout-for-my-office.html). Panel heights 42"/53-54"/65"/85" (https://www.officefurniture2go.com/answers/cubicles-workstations/what-panel-heights-are-available-for-cubicles.html) — use 1.37 m and 1.65 m; the target reads ~1.5 m. Build islands as 2x2 pinwheels (4 stations, 3.7-4.9 m square) or 2x3 back-to-back rows; leave one island side open toward the main aisle so desks and CRTs are visible (T shows open-ended stations facing the aisle).

**R4. Aisles.** Primary circulation 44-48" (1.12-1.22 m) per the same planning page; search summaries cite 60" (1.5 m) preferred and 36-42" (0.9-1.07 m) secondary, 30" (0.76 m) chair rollback behind each desk (UNVERIFIED beyond the search summary). For a chase game, make the *gameplay* aisle 2.0-2.5 m (wider than real; it is the door-to-door axis protected by `keepClear`) and use real 0.9-1.1 m aisles between islands so those read as tight side paths.

**R5. Columns.** Square, 0.6-0.75 m, wall-coloured, on a regular bay; T shows a column row flanking the central aisle. In the 3 m maze, put columns at every 3rd cell corner (9.1 m = 30 ft bay, a typical office span — UNVERIFIED) and never inside `keepClear`; Fandom 4.3 allows occasional **clusters** of closely grouped load-bearing walls as landmarks.

**R6. Perimeter, not floating.** Vending machine, water cooler, copier, filing cabinets, fountain go **against walls, column faces or island ends**, back within 5-10 cm of the surface, slightly off-square (1-3°). This is how T reads (cooler + copier against a cubicle end and wall; vending against the wall).

**R7. Windows.** At most 1-2 visible per room; interior windows in partition walls of enclosed rooms (sill ~0.9 m, head ~2.1 m, typ.), glass rendered near-black with a faint reflection; exterior windows blacked out or rain-on-fog. Canon: W L4, W L11, F L4.

**R8. Doors and depth.** Door openings on the room's long axis with a lit corridor or next room beyond (T: wood-framed doorway into a long corridor with repeated fixtures) — depth through repetition is the strongest Backrooms cue.

**R9. Ceiling heights.** Real offices ~9 ft (2.74 m) lay-in ceilings (BPA guide below). FrontRooms zones: 2.4 m = cramped back office (lower partitions 1.37 m); 2.9 m = standard office; 5.4 m = do **not** use cubicles alone (they look like dollhouse props) — use it for lobby/atrium, furniture piles (Red's still B pile is ~2.5 m), or add a lowered soffit.

**R10. Human residue, not clutter.** Each station gets at most 3 small items (paper stack, keyboard, mug/phone); 1 in ~4 chairs missing; 1 in ~6 monitors on. Add carpet **furniture-ghost decals** (Fandom L4 indents) in empty halls to imply removed cubicles.

**R11. Streamed room train (11.5 x 12 x 2.9 m).** Fits one 2x2 island each side of a 2.5 m centre aisle plus a wall run of appliances, i.e. T's composition; keep the door axis clear and put the window on a side wall.

## 8. Lighting rules

Real baseline — BPA "Open Office" LED troffer layout guide (text extracted from https://bpa.gov/-/media/Aep/energy-efficiency/trade-ally-resources/trade-ally-network-nw/lighting-open-office-led-troffer.pdf , rev. 10/2018): 60'x25' room, typical 9' lay-in ceiling; reflectances **ceiling 80%, walls 50%, floor 20%**; 2'x4' lensed troffers; **8'x10' spacing, 18 fixtures, ~38 fc at 30" AFF**; 8'x12' spacing gives ~33 fc; IES recommendation **30-50 fc**; with 4-6 ft partitions, fixtures should sit **over workstations**.

Backrooms canon modifiers:
- **L1 Not all lights work.** Fandom Level 4: many lights do not function and the switches are missing; large rooms dark, small rooms lit (image caption). Kane's tile survey treats the troffer itself as an object of study. => Place fixtures on the full real grid, then light only 30-60% (seeded), plus 1 flickering per ~3 rooms (Level 1 "flickering" blackouts, https://backrooms-wiki.wikidot.com/level-1 lead).
- **L2 Light from fixtures only.** The film placed no film lights and used practicals (`VISUAL_RESEARCH_LOOKDEV.md` citing ShotDeck/Sony interviews). In-game: no fill lights; ambient comes from bounce (ceiling 0.8 / wall 0.5 / carpet 0.2 albedo targets, from BPA reflectances).
- **L3 Gross on purpose.** Kane remembers 90s photos where the flash is on and white balance is off (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backrooms_(web_series)). The grade should be off-white-balance (green-yellow for Level 0, green-grey for Office as in T), not a clean neutral.
- **L4 Haze for depth.** T has distant haze; F4.3 has permanent white fog; Push Square credits ETB's lighting + fog for the vibe (https://www.pushsquare.com/reviews/ps5/escape-the-backrooms). Use subtle volumetric/height fog so the 3rd-4th room down the axis falls off.
- **L5 Dim, uneven.** Red's target is well below 38 fc: one line of lit panels over the main aisle, others off; islands sit in falloff. Aim for an exposure where lit-lens = highlight, carpet under a lit fixture = mid-grey, corners near black.
- **L6 Practical emissives on props.** A few CRT screens (dim blue/grey), vending rows (warm), exit signs (red/green) give colour accents in an otherwise cool room.
- Implementation note (engineering, not canon): rooms are recycled at runtime, so baking is not available per instance; prefer a capped number of real-time lights per room (e.g. lit fixtures as shadowless spot/area approximations, 1-2 shadow casters), dead fixtures as emissive-off meshes, and SSAO for contact grounding.

## 10. What reads as cheap vs. convincing (implications for FrontRooms)

Evidence base: the current build frame `Frontrooms3D/Verification/lookdev/2_Office_forward.png` (viewed 2026-10-02: cubicle "islands" read as long beige slabs with dark sides, no panels/monitors legible, one big 2x4 parabolic-grid fixture, a black wall rectangle as the window) vs. Red's target `ref_office_target.png`; plus the game observations in section 4.

Reads **cheap** (seen in the current build and in low-effort Backrooms games):
1. Box primitives with flat colour and no bevels — edges never catch the troffer light, so nothing has a silhouette (current Office slabs).
2. No material separation — fabric, laminate, painted steel and ABS all share one roughness; the eye cannot tell what anything is made of.
3. Wrong scale/proportion — desks too low/long, panels as thick as walls. Real dimensions (section 7) are the cheapest realism upgrade.
4. Perfect alignment and identical copies — every chair square to every desk, every island identical; Backrooms "copy-paste" should be *one* uncanny layer, not the baseline.
5. Props not grounded — no contact shadow/AO, no carpet compression, floating 1-2 cm.
6. Over-lit, all fixtures on, no haze — kills depth; canon wants many dead lights.
7. Clutter for its own sake or random junk — canon offices are *sparse*; noise reads as asset flip.
8. Anachronisms — flat panels, LED troffers, laptops, modern mesh chairs.
9. Uniform tiling textures with visible repeats on carpet and ceiling; no macro variation (stains in clusters, traffic lanes).

Reads **convincing** (target image, POOLS/Anemoiapolis/Exit 8 approach, canon cues):
1. Few hero props modelled to real dimensions with bevels, sub-parts (panel top caps, desk modesty panel, CRT bezel/vents, chair gas lift), and 3-5 materials each.
2. Correct reflectance: ceiling ~0.8, walls ~0.5, carpet ~0.2 (BPA), plastics beige 0.55-0.65, black chairs 0.04-0.06 (UNVERIFIED albedo ranges, standard PBR practice).
3. Wear where humans touch: desk front edges, chair arms, carpet traffic lanes, scuffs at chair-back height, yellowed CRT tops (UV side).
4. Human irregularity: chairs rotated, a drawer open, one monitor on, paper fanned.
5. Emptiness with traces: carpet indents/ghost rectangles where furniture was removed (Fandom L4) — absence that implies presence.
6. Depth by repetition + haze through door openings (T, Kane corridor shots).
7. Dead and dim fixtures, one flicker, cool off-white-balance grade (T; Kane quote).
8. Anomalies are *intact* objects doing impossible things (stretched, sunk, piled), using the same hero meshes as the normal office so the player recognises them.
9. Photo-referenced materials (Exit 8: own photos -> models; POOLS: restraint) beat procedural flat colour.
