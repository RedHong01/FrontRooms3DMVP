# 01 — Backrooms IP entities: how they look, move and are filmed (Hunter redesign research)

Status: COMPLETE (2026-10-02). Written incrementally; all sections filled.
Scope: Kane Pixels' series (Found Footage entity, later entities, Async framing), the A24 film (2026), the Backrooms Wiki's common entities (as a vocabulary of ideas; licence), fan games' monsters. Goal: extract a visual grammar for The Relay's model redesign and list what NOT to copy.

Conventions: every factual claim carries the URL it was read from. "UNVERIFIED" = search-snippet only, or not confirmed on a fetched page. At most one short quote per source. Interpretations are marked as "Reading:".

Project constraints this research serves (from `LEVEL_MODULE_SPEC.md` §7, `RELAY_MODEL_RIG_RESEARCH.md`, the Hunter code): body r 0.3; ≤ 2.05 m tall while walking (else stoop) to pass a 1.0 × 2.1 m door and 2.4 m ceilings; eye/sight ray at 1.60 m; states Listen/Search, Hunt (walk), Chase (run 4.2–5.4 m/s), BreakDoor, Stagger; it breaks doors, hunts by noise, and "relays" closer when unseen. Current language: tall, hunched, blank pale head with a narrow dark face void, charcoal body, muted yellow detail; Red finds the current rig spindly/odd.

## 0. TL;DR

1. **Kane's first monster (the "Lifeform").** It is a very tall, dark, wire- or vine-built humanoid. Its arms reach the floor and it bends far forward. It is seen in glimpses through camcorder glitches and blur, and announced by an edited chimpanzee scream. The fan games cloned it (Escape the Backrooms' "Bacteria", also nicknamed "Howler"; Inside the Backrooms' "Cable Monster"), so this is the Backrooms monster shape to stay away from.
2. **The A24 film's monster.** It is **Pirate Clark**, a "Still Life": the place's misremembered copy of Clark in his furniture-store pirate-mascot costume.
   - The performer is 7'7" and the creature is mostly practical (Amazing Ape / Werner Pretorius makeup effects). The face (Zoic) and the peg leg are CG.
   - The animators kept fear and sadness on the face, not snarls. The performer plays it as deliberate, never twitchy.
   - It is located by a unique peg-leg footstep. Its voice is the actor's anguish spliced with camel calls.
3. **The IP's entity grammar.** These are principles we can use freely:
   - a human outline with one or two countable errors;
   - a misremembered copy of something ordinary from the same era as the set;
   - seen first through media artefacts, then late and in full;
   - sound first, with one signature footstep;
   - framed by doorways and hiding in architecture;
   - deliberate rather than twitchy, and pitiable rather than snarling;
   - lights and the camera react to it.
4. **The wiki is a vocabulary, not a source.** Its text is CC BY-SA 3.0, and using it forces a CC BY-SA 3.0 or GPLv3 release. Each entity's art belongs to a named artist. Ideas are free. Use principles only, never names, text or art.
5. **Warning about the current Relay.** Its charcoal body, blank pale head, face slot, long arms and hunch overlap the wiki's **Dullers** (dark-grey, featureless, long-armed), the Lifeform (hunch, dark value) and Facelings (blank face). The parts are not copied, but together they read as a generic Backrooms monster, which supports Red's "spindly/odd" note.
6. **Direction for concepts.** Within the ≤ 2.05 m walking height, 1.0 × 2.1 m door and r 0.3 limits:
   - make it read as too big for the room, with mass in the upper body on a human frame;
   - base it on a misremembered late-1980s/early-1990s person or object, with no hazmat, no mascot and no costume;
   - design the silhouette to read inside a lit doorway;
   - give it one specific light element, one signature footstep, and post/lamp reactions on first sight;
   - avoid the list in §6.

## 1. Kane Pixels' "The Backrooms" series

Primary facts come from Wikipedia's series page (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backrooms_(web_series)). Morphology comes from the **fan-run Kane Pixels Backrooms Wiki** on Fandom (read in the built-in browser, 2026-10-02, because WebFetch gets HTTP 402 there). That wiki is a secondary, fan-written source: its descriptions are screen readings plus Kane's Discord posts, so treat them as "what viewers see", not production fact.

### 1.1 The Found Footage entity (ep. 1, 2022-01-07): "the Lifeform"

**What happens.** The cameraman hears a sound behind him and turns to see a creature, which Parsons calls "the Lifeform"; it pursues him across floors, charges, grabs him and he drops the camera (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backrooms_(web_series)). Before the encounter he finds graffiti of eyes and a face and the phrase "don't move stay still"; a noise echoes, **the camera glitches**, and he sees a shadowy, monstrous figure to his left (https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/the-backrooms-found-footage). It is later seen throwing a chair at a wall to drive him on, follows him into a wall opening and grabs him by a sloped pit (https://kane-pixels-backrooms.fandom.com/wiki/The_Lifeform).

**What is visible (fan wiki reading of the frames).** https://kane-pixels-backrooms.fandom.com/wiki/The_Lifeform:
- Very tall: the wiki presumes about 3.35 m (11 ft) if fully upright, and notes it bends its torso far forward in Found Footage.
- Two long arm-like limbs that reach the ground, thin skinny fingers.
- A slim, vine-like anatomy, like a tangle of black wires in a humanoid shape.
- It reads black on screen only because of the VHS look and Kane's grading; the wiki says the model is actually fleshy red.
- Implied to be a colony of mutated hay bacillus (fans call it "Bacteria"; Kane's Ko-fi item is titled that, per the same page).
- A blog explainer describes it the same way: humanoid, dark, vine-like, elongated limbs and a bent torso (https://taylorholmes.com/2026/05/29/explaining-the-backrooms-youtube-videos-prior-to-watching-the-movie/).

**Sound.** The Found Footage screams are **edited chimpanzee audio** (Wikipedia, via search snippet of https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backrooms_(web_series); the fetched summary did not surface that sentence, and the fan wiki repeats it: https://kane-pixels-backrooms.fandom.com/wiki/The_Lifeform). Later episodes use a stock effect of a child screaming, and the creature mimics human pleas for help (fan wiki, same page). While chasing, its calls are described as wailing, horn-like, and like a metal chair dragged on the floor (fan wiki, same page).

**Behaviour.** Fan wiki (same page): wanders, nests in secluded areas among tendrils like its body, wakes when a human enters, charges on detection and runs at about the victim's speed until it loses sight of them or catches them; hunts by sound, so silence alone does not save a cornered victim. A Discord-sourced note says a T-pose phasing through a wall in the Corridors chase was "bad animation" Kane would not call canon.

**Reading.** The first Backrooms monster that most people saw is defined by (a) a reach-to-the-floor arm length, (b) a forward-bent torso, (c) a thin, wiry, non-solid body, (d) near-black value from the media, not the material, and (e) an animal-sourced scream. Our current Relay already sits close to (a)–(b) and (d): the "spindly, odd" feel Red dislikes is partly the Lifeform's own grammar.

### 1.3 How the series films its entities (media artefacts)

- **Found-footage camcorder.** The series is presented as found footage with a VHS look; Parsons made it in Blender and After Effects, later adding motion-capture suits for character animation (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backrooms_(web_series)).
- **The creature arrives with an artefact.** The camera glitches at the moment of the reveal (https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/the-backrooms-found-footage).
- **Blurred.** Dazed describes the first video's turn as "signs of a blurred-out creature" starting the chase (https://dazeddigital.com/film-tv/article/62125/1/a24-youngest-ever-director-kane-parsons-the-backrooms-creepypasta-into-cinema).
- **Imperfection is authored.** A search summary says Kane simulates the cameraman's shaky hands, digital noise and VHS tracking errors even though it is CG. The Dazed page does not say this; source not found → UNVERIFIED (it matches what the videos show, but cite it only as a reading).
- **Brief and partial.** It is on screen long enough to read as wrong, not long enough to study (https://taylorholmes.com/2026/05/29/explaining-the-backrooms-youtube-videos-prior-to-watching-the-movie/). A motion camera only catches waving appendages (https://kane-pixels-backrooms.fandom.com/wiki/The_Lifeform).
- **Value from the medium.** The red model turns black through VHS distortion and grading (fan wiki, same page). Reading: the silhouette, not the surface, is what the audience gets.
- **Sound before sight.** A sound behind him makes him turn (Wikipedia, above); a scream announces it (https://eikonproject.substack.com/p/caught-in-a-backroom-nightmare).

### 1.4 Async framing: hazmat suits, research, documents

- Async researchers wear **Class A hazmat suits** with M17 gas masks and oxygen tanks inside a baggy suit; supervisors' suits are mostly green; a "combat" variant is less baggy with tank and mask outside, a utility belt and a CB radio (https://kane-pixels-backrooms.fandom.com/wiki/Async_Hazmat_Suits).
- The fan wiki notes Kane said on Discord the suits are hard to run in, and that masks limit sight (same page).
- The suit models are a third-party Blender asset ("Hazmat Suit Nbc" by Albin BKB); the film's suits are practical recreations of that model (same page; fan claim, UNVERIFIED against the asset page).
- Async treats the place as a research site: corpses covered in mould and mutated hay bacillus are logged and autopsied, motion cameras are set out, a captured creature ends on a dissection table with scans (https://kane-pixels-backrooms.fandom.com/wiki/The_Lifeform ; https://kane-pixels-backrooms.fandom.com/wiki/Pirate_Clark).
- The informational videos read as 1980s corporate promo and VHS (03_ip_canon.md, citing https://taylorholmes.com/2026/05/29/explaining-the-backrooms-youtube-videos-prior-to-watching-the-movie/).

**Reading.** In this IP, protective gear and documentation are part of the creature design: the threat is defined by the procedures around it (labels, bait cut-outs, gas traps, autopsy scans). A hazmat-suited figure is the most "Async" silhouette there is, which is exactly why The Relay must not be one (see §6).

## 2. The A24 film *Backrooms* (2026)

### 2.1 What the threat is

- The film's creatures are the **Still Lifes**: the Backrooms recreates people from memory as it recreates rooms, and gets them wrong (https://www.thewrap.com/creative-content/movies/backrooms-ending-lore-explained/). Clark's line: the place "remembers" them, and the more it remembers something, the less it does (https://en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Backrooms, via 01_film_production.md S17).
- The main antagonist is **Pirate Clark** ("Captain Clark"): a towering, distorted copy of Clark dressed as his furniture store's pirate mascot, which kills Clark and chases Mary (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backrooms_(film)). Played by **Robert Bobroczkyi** (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backrooms_(film)), who is 7'7"; /Film calls the creature "an almost eight foot tall" homunculus (https://slashfilm.com/2184275/backrooms-movie-alien-franchise-connection-robert-bobroczkyi). VFX Voice gives 8.5 ft (https://vfxvoice.com/where-practical-ends-and-vfx-begins-in-backrooms/).
- Its form copies the costume: a right arm jutting at an angle, mimicking the crutch from the commercial, and a peg leg (https://www.thewrap.com/creative-content/movies/backrooms-ending-lore-explained/). Fan wiki detail: torso tilted hard to one side; peg leg like a furniture chair leg; extra intersecting fingers; duplicated eye and nose; a frowning mouth stretched sideways (https://kane-pixels-backrooms.fandom.com/wiki/Pirate_Clark).
- Behaviour (fan wiki, same page): heard as bangs, thuds and footsteps before it is seen; steps out of doorways; tears an Async bait cut-out apart; drags a victim back by his own rope; **breaks a door down** to follow Mary; is finally brought down by hitting the peg leg; captured, it is shown on an autopsy table.
- Other Still Lifes in the film look almost human, with distortion mainly in hands and face, and white stuffing-like insides (https://kane-pixels-backrooms.fandom.com/wiki/Still_Lifes). Wikipedia's plot calls them made of "an edible white substance" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backrooms_(film)).

### 2.2 How it was designed and made (practical / VFX, people, interviews)

From VFX Voice (https://vfxvoice.com/where-practical-ends-and-vfx-begins-in-backrooms/), unless noted:
- **Makeup-effects first.** Makeup Effects Supervisor **Werner Pretorius** with **Amazing Ape** built more creatures than expected; VFX supervisor Edward Douglas says the result was more practical than he expected. Other vendors asked for the un-retouched plates because they took the prosthetics for CG. (Wikipedia also credits Amazing Ape Productions and Pretorius for creature FX: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backrooms_(film).)
- **Redheaded Still Life:** inspired by sculptor **Evan Penny**, whose faces look smeared or distorted from different angles; worn by Rhiannon Roberts for three days.
- **Striped-shirt Still Life:** about 90% prosthetic face with depthless glass eyes; VFX made the extra eyes blink offset, in a cascade.
- **Pirate Clark:** a real tall performer was chosen over stilts; practical body, costume and hat throughout, puppeteer Richard Darwin; **Zoic Studios** replaced only the face with CG built from a scan of Chiwetel Ejiofor after digital-makeup tests fell short; the **peg leg is entirely CG** for stunt safety.
- **Expression, not snarl.** Early scary-face passes with furrowed brows and snarls were rejected; animators kept **fear and sadness** and micro-adjusted it shot to shot.
- **Sound** (https://www.motionpictures.org/2026/06/inside-the-sound-of-backrooms-how-camel-screams-clipped-audio-empty-rooms-fueled-the-fear/): sound designer **Eugenio Battaglia** built the creature voice from Ejiofor's anguished grunts and crying, pitched, spliced with **camel** sounds; the target texture was "crunchy", like something made of wood or twigs; **the peg-leg footstep** is the film's recurring cue that an entity is coming; he also used mic clipping to break the audience's defences.
- **Practical, with CG only where needed** (https://bloody-disgusting.com/editorials/3954939/meet-the-actors-who-brought-the-backrooms-still-life-monsters-to-life-spoilers/): every Still Life is a performer in heavy makeup and prosthetics, with occasional CGI; Pirate Clark's CG is the peg leg and the face. The same piece notes the film opens with a found-footage prologue (Naren Warne, played by Avan Jogia), which is where the creature is first met.
- **Performer on movement** (A24 making-of clip, reported at https://bloody-disgusting.com/movie/3961059/backrooms-behind-the-scenes-video-shows-how-robert-bobroczkyi-brought-pirate-clark-to-life/): Bobroczkyi plays it as a creature that knows its body and limits, so its actions are deliberate; "He doesn't just twitch for no reason." The prosthetics are two hand pieces plus a heavy helmet/face/head piece he can barely see out of.
- **Where it is first seen** (fan wiki, https://kane-pixels-backrooms.fandom.com/wiki/Pirate_Clark): as an obscured figure in a dark room on Bobby's camcorder, then by sound (bangs, thuds, footsteps) long before its full reveal; the full-light reveal is saved for the last act. Everything Must Go (ep. 25, 2026-08-18) shows a tall humanoid entity in a pirate costume (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backrooms_(web_series)).
- The found-footage parts of the film were printed to VHS after grade and VFX; the rest is clean digital (https://theasc.com/article/backrooms-cinematography-cox/, via 01_film_production.md S10).
- Parsons' own Fangoria interview does not discuss the creatures (https://www.fangoria.com/director-kane-parsons-bizarre-backrooms-interview/).

**Reading.** The film's monster grammar is: a copy of a person that is *almost right* (proportion and duplication errors, not gore), shown in a real, lit set; the horror is the sad, frightened face on a body that will still kill you; and it is located by a unique footstep. It is tall but built on a human frame, not a stick figure.

**Reading.**
- The fan games converged on Kane's Lifeform as "the Level 0 monster" (ETB's Bacteria, Inside's Cable Monster). A thin, black, wire-built humanoid is now the genre default, so a Relay in that shape reads as a knock-off of a knock-off.

## 5. The IP's visual grammar for entities

Ten rules, each tied to the evidence above. "Reading:" lines are this report's interpretation.

1. **Humanoid, but wrong in one or two measurable ways.** Kane's Lifeform is a human outline built of the wrong material, with arms to the floor (§1.1). The Still Lifes are human bodies with errors in proportion and duplication: a short leg, a misshapen head, extra fingers, a doubled eye (§1.2, §2.1). The wiki's humanoids are people minus one thing: no face, no breath, no blinking (§3.2). Reading: the wrongness is a *countable* error (a length, a count, a missing feature), not texture or gore.
2. **A copy of something ordinary from the place's own world.** The film's monster is a copy of a man in his store-mascot costume, assembled from the costume's props (crutch arm, peg leg) (§2.1). The Still Lifes copy people the way the rooms copy rooms (§2.1). Facelings copy jobs, and Stalkers copy hikers (§3.2). Reading: the strongest IP entities are *misremembered ordinary things from the same era as the set*, which matches FrontRooms' late-1980s to early-1990s period and copy-paste furniture logic.
3. **Seen through the medium first.** It arrives with a camera glitch, is blurred, and reads black because of VHS grading (§1.1, §1.3). The film introduces its monster in a camcorder prologue, and its found-footage sections were printed to VHS (§2.2). In ETB the image glitches and adds static while you look at it (§4). Reading: the first reads are a silhouette plus an artefact; the surface detail is a late reward.
4. **Sound before sight, and one signature sound.** A noise behind you makes you turn (§1.1). The film's entity is located by its peg-leg footstep throughout, and its voice was built from a human performance plus camel calls (§2.2). Kane's scream is edited chimpanzee audio (§1.1). The 1998 game hunts by your microphone (§4). Reading: give the creature one footstep nobody else in the game makes.
5. **It mimics or lures with ordinary sounds.** The Lifeform imitates pleas for help (§1.1). ETB's Bacteria knocks (§4). Skin-Stealers repeat speech (§3.2). Reading: knocking and voice mimicry are now common enough to read as a quotation; FrontRooms' noise-hunting can stay one-way (it listens, it does not call).
6. **It blends with architecture or hides in it.** Windows are architecture (§3.2). Dullers reach through walls (§3.2). Pirate Clark steps out of doorways and walks into a sideways door (§2.1). The Lifeform nests in tendrils like its own body (§1.1). Reading: the doorway is the IP's frame for a reveal. FrontRooms' 1.0 × 2.1 m door is the creature's picture frame, so design the silhouette to read inside that rectangle.
7. **Brief, partial, dark, then a full reveal late.** The Lifeform is on screen just long enough (§1.3). Smilers are only eyes and teeth (§3.2). Pirate Clark is an obscured figure for most of the film (§2.2). Reading: the model needs a strong read at 10–20 m in a doorway at low light, *and* survive a close look once.
8. **Deliberate, not twitchy.** The film's performer plays it as deliberate and not twitching (§2.2). Per the fan wiki, Kane called the Lifeform's T-pose wall-phase "bad animation" (§1.1). Reading: the jitter-and-glitch school of monster motion is not this IP's best register; weight, planted feet and purpose are.
9. **Pitiable, not snarling.** The film rejected furrowed-brow snarls and kept fear and sadness on the face (§2.2). Facelings and Still Lifes are failed attempts at people (§2.1, §3.2). Reading: menace comes from scale, persistence and what it does, not from a scary face.
10. **Light and media react to it.** A Still Life flicks the room lights off (§1.2). Inside's Cable Monster turns lights red (§4). Smilers and Deathmoths are drawn to light (§3.2). Reading: lamp behaviour is part of the creature's design kit, and FrontRooms already has per-fixture ballast logic to hang this on.

## 6. What to AVOID copying

Never copy or closely imitate these. Principles from §5 are free; these specific designs are not (Kane's series and the film are all rights reserved; wiki art belongs to named artists under CC BY-SA or other licences, §3.1).

**Kane Pixels / A24 (all rights reserved)**
- **The Lifeform's silhouette:** a black, wire- or vine-built humanoid with floor-length arms, thin fingers and a forward-bent torso. Fan games already copied it (ETB's Bacteria with its holed chunky head; Inside's black "Cable Monster"), so it is the most recognisable Backrooms monster shape (§1.1, §4).
- **Pirate Clark:** the mascot or costume concept, a tricorne or any brimmed hat, a peg leg or furniture-leg limb, a crutch-shaped arm, a torso tilted hard to one side, duplicated eyes, noses or mouths, extra intersecting fingers, rings. Also avoid its signature cue: an asymmetric wooden footstep (§2.1, §2.2).
- **Still Lifes:** Evan Penny-style faces smeared across angles, cascading multiple blinking eyes, white stuffing inside, a person fused to furniture (wheelchair, lamp) (§2.2). Also avoid the FF3 Still Life's combination of one short leg, a misshapen head, and knocking before breaking the door (§1.2).
- **Async hazmat suits:** the Class A suit with an M17 mask and tank, green supervisor suits, the Async logo, and the third-party "Hazmat Suit Nbc" model they come from (§1.4). Any hazmat-suited monster reads as Async.
- **Names:** Lifeform, Bacteria, Still Life, Pirate/Captain/Cap'n Clark, Async.

**Backrooms Wiki (CC BY-SA 3.0 text; art by named artists)**
- Smilers (glowing eyes and a wide toothed grin in the dark); Hounds (long hair hanging over the face, a human on all fours with an oversized mouth); Skin-Stealers (a pale-yellow body in worn human skin, sucker-bump skin); Facelings (blank face as the whole idea); Partygoers (yellow skin, carved smiley, noodle arms with mouths); Wretches (pustuled, flaky skin and misplaced eyes); Dullers (dark-grey, frail, featureless, long extendable arms); Windows; Stalkers (§3.2).
- Names: Smiler, Hound, Skin-Stealer, Faceling, Partygoer, Wretch, Deathmoth, Duller, Howler, Almond Water, "=)".

**The overlap problem with the current Relay.** Its palette and parts (charcoal `#2B2928` body, blank pale head, narrow dark face slot, long arms, hunch, uneven shoulders; `FrontRooms3DGame.cs` line 227 and `FrontRoomsRelayRig.BuildRig`) already sit between Dullers (dark grey, featureless, long arms), the Lifeform (hunch, long arms, dark value) and Facelings (blank face). None of it is copied, but the result reads as *generic Backrooms monster*, which may be part of why it feels "odd" rather than specific. The rig's bone offsets also put a 0.62 m neck segment between chest and head (at `bodyScale` 1), the longest single segment above the pelvis, which adds to the spindly read.

## 7. Implications for The Relay (input to the concept phase)

These are research constraints for the concepts, not a design.

**Fixed by the project** (`LEVEL_MODULE_SPEC.md` §7; `FrontRoomsModuleUnits.cs` `RelayRadius .3, RelayHeight 2.05, RelayEye 1.6`):
- Body radius 0.3 m, probed between 0.4 and 1.95 m. At most 2.05 m tall while walking, so it passes a 1.0 × 2.1 m door and 2.4 m ceilings. Sight ray at 1.60 m.
- States: Listen/Search, Hunt (walk), Chase (run 4.2–5.4 m/s), BreakDoor, Stagger. It hunts by noise and relays closer when unseen.

**What the IP research says to do with that**
1. **Tall through the room, not through raw height.** The IP's hunters are 2.3–3.4 m (Pirate Clark's performer is 7'7"; the Lifeform is presumed 11 ft). The Relay is capped at 2.05 m walking, so make it read as *too big for the room*: shoulders that nearly fill the 1.0 m door, a stoop that the 2.1 m door head visibly forces, and an upright Listen pose that, if allowed, rises above the walking cap under the 2.4 m ceiling (to be agreed with the map chat; the probe stops at 1.95 m).
2. **Leave the thin-wire family.** Red's "spindly" note and §6 point the same way: give it mass in the shoulders and upper body on a human frame, with the wrongness in one or two countable places (§5 rule 1).
3. **Make it a misremembered period object or person** from FrontRooms' own late-1980s to early-1990s world (§5 rule 2). Candidate sources: office workwear, building services, furniture (the game already uses copy-paste furniture). Exclude hazmat, mascot and costume ideas (§6).
4. **Design the silhouette for the doorway.** Test the first read as a dark mid-value shape inside a lit 1.0 × 2.1 m frame against mono-yellow paper and against the green-grey Office grade (§5 rules 3, 6, 7).
5. **One light element is enough.** The current pale head already gives "a doorway or flicker can reveal it" (`RELAY_MODEL_RIG_RESEARCH.md`). Keep a single high-value read, but make it *specific*, not a blank face (Facelings), a grin (Smilers) or glowing eyes.
6. **One signature footstep.** The Relay's heavier carpet Foley already exists (`RELAY_MODEL_RIG_RESEARCH.md`). Give it a material identity tied to the new body, and avoid the film's wooden peg-leg step (§5 rule 4, §6).
7. **Deliberate motion.** Walk and run with weight and planted feet. Reserve wrongness for one joint or timing beat. No idle jitter (§5 rule 8).
8. **Media and lights react.** On first sight: a short grain or chromatic-aberration bump in the existing post stack, and nearby troffers stutter. This is cheap and on-grammar (§5 rules 3, 10). Not a full glitch-screen.
9. **Not a jump-in.** Reveal it in a doorway at distance and let the chase follow. Do not fill the screen with a scream (PSU's criticism of The Backrooms 1998, §4).
10. **Keep the IP's restraint as a production advantage.** The best-regarded entities are seen briefly and partially, so a modest mesh with a strong silhouette fits the WebGL budget in `RELAY_MODEL_RIG_RESEARCH.md` (24–36 bones, 4 influences).
