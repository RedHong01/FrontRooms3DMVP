# FrontRooms Level Designer (P1–P4)

Author a room as data, see it in the game's own build next to the editor, and let the generator place it in the real maze. Every number follows `LEVEL_MODULE_SPEC.md`.

## Use it

1. **FrontRooms → Level Designer → Open** (or press *Open in Level Designer* in a room module's Inspector). This opens `Assets/Scenes/FrontRoomsLevelDesigner.unity` on the module and the **Level Designer window**, docked as a tab beside the Inspector (floating if no Inspector is open). **FrontRooms → Level Designer → Window** opens the window alone.
2. **Left: the preview.** The Scene view frames the room; the Game view shows the eye camera standing in its first opening. The room is built by the game's own map code, in a maze whose zones take the room's height and theme, with lamps, columns, the Office kit, piles and the Office grade exactly as in the game (the generator's own modules are left out of the preview, so every tagged prop is this room's). The Scene view is also an editor: see *In the Scene view*.
3. **Right: the window** is the design panel. Every edit rebuilds the preview: a drag or a slider when you let go. In Play too, when the running designer scene's preview shows this module: only the room's chunk of the running map is rebuilt and you stay where you stand (back at the entrance only if the edit put a prop, a wall or a column where you stood); a new size (of the room as turned), height or theme, or a new preview seed or profile, rebuilds the whole map and starts you at the entrance. Any other module's edits are saved but do not reach the running map (the window says which). A module's own Inspector still edits it with the same plan and sections (no module list, palette or Scene selection sync there).
4. **Play here** (window, Preview section) or Play in the designer scene walks the room (WASD, mouse, Shift, E). Keep editing in the window while you walk (see 3); the Scene view tools and the preview controls are off in Play.

New rooms: **New** in the window or **FrontRooms → Level Designer → New room module** (saved in `Assets/Levels/Modules`). Samples: **Create sample modules** (a Level 0 waiting room, an Office bullpen, a tall pillar hall, a low store room) makes the missing ones and never overwrites; **Reset sample modules** puts them back as shipped (asks first).

## The window

| Section | What it does |
|---|---|
| Modules | every room module asset, with a search field; **New**, **Duplicate**, **Rename**, **Ping**. Picking one shows it in the preview (undoable on the preview) and frames it; the pick survives script reloads and Play. A row says *generator* when the level profile uses the module |
| Room | notes; **Width, Depth** in 3 m cells (1–8); **Theme, Ceiling** Level 0 or Office (Office is Standard height), Low 2.4 / Standard 2.9 / Tall 5.4 m; **Fill** **Auto** as a generated room (Office kit in Offices, sometimes a pile in Level 0 halls ≥ 4 × 4), **None** only your props, **Office** the Office kit fills round your props, **Pile** a furniture pile; **Columns** **Auto** the map's 6 m grid rule if the room wins its column roll (pale squares in the plan: where they stand with the room turned as in the preview; the generator lands the room on the same 6 m grid phase, so that turn matches the game, but it may use another allowed turn, which can move them to the other inner corners; use **Custom** for columns that must stay put), **None**, **Custom** click inner corners (Shift: 0.9 m) |
| Plan | north up. Click an edge: wall → arch (doorway) → open. Right-click a cell: its lamp (Auto, Steady, Stutter, Failing, Dead, Dim, Off), or **Key spot here** / **Relay entry here** (see *Markers*). Green strips: floor kept clear inside openings. Props: click to select (again: the one underneath), drag to move (0.05 m snap, Ctrl 0.5 m, an axis only once the mouse takes it to another step, so a wall unit slid along its wall keeps its gap; the preview rebuilds when you let go, a click that only selects leaves it alone), R turns 90°, Delete removes, Esc deselects. The selection stays on its prop when props are removed or reordered elsewhere or undone. The dark band on a prop is its front. With a kit armed, a click places it (Shift-click keeps it armed, Esc stops); a green ghost shows where it will stand. Markers are drawn over the props and picked before them |
| Palette | every kit asset with its thumbnail, **All / Floor / Wall / DeskTop** and a search field (hover a tile for its size). Click a kit to arm it (again to disarm), or drag it into the plan or onto the floor in the Scene view. Where it lands: floor kits at the point, snapped to 0.05 m; **wall units** with their back 3 cm off the nearest wall face (the room's walls and inner walls, a cell edge at a time; doorways and openings are not walls), centred where they were put, their whole footprint on the run of wall they stand against and clear of the corner, doorway or crossing wall at each end; hung pieces (a *hang* anchor: the clock) 2.1 m up; **desk-top items** on the top of the prop under them (a desk, a cabinet), turned with it, without a collider (on the floor if there is no top). New kits exported from Blender show after the import, without a script reload |
| Props | the module's props in placing order: click a row to select, ▲ ▼ reorder, ✕ removes. Under it the selected prop's fields: kit, X/Z, height above the floor (wall pieces: clock, interior window), yaw, *No collider* for clutter; only the value you change snaps to 0.05 m, and a slider rebuilds the preview when you let go; Turn 90°, Duplicate, Remove |
| Markers | the module's gameplay markers, a row each (click selects it in the plan, ✕ removes it); **Key spot** and **Relay entry** arm one for the plan. Under it the selected marker's fields: kind, X/Z, yaw; a key spot's height, **On the prop under it** and host; a Relay entry's tag. See *Markers* |
| Preview | **Seed** and **Turn** (quarter turns clockwise, as the generator may place it) of the preview, **Rebuild**, **Frame**, **Play here** (opens the designer scene on the module if needed and enters Play mode). Off in Play |
| Generator | **Used by the generator** adds the module to the level profile's *Modules* or takes it out; the level's **module chance** and **module tier** (the profile's generation numbers; the module tier is the one at run tier 1, and each run tier above it adds one); the module's weight, may rotate and tier range. Profile edits are undoable and mark `Assets/Levels/FrontRoomsLevel0.asset` dirty (save the project to keep them). It warns when the module tier over a whole run (module tier to module tier + last run tier − 1) misses the module's range, says at which run tiers it comes when only some reach it, and warns when the weight is 0, or the module never or rarely fits: the generator only puts it into carved rooms of its height that it fits in an allowed turn, each side rolled between that height's *Room Min* and *Room Max* cells (at most 8); under a quarter of those sizes is "rarely". The same fit line shows in the module's Inspector |
| Checks | **errors**: no way in, inner walls cutting cells off, a prop in a wall or above the ceiling, a prop with a collider in an opening's 1.0 m clear strip (the map leaves it out of the build), props blocking an opening or cutting part of the room off (a 0.25 m walk test with the player's 0.3 m body); **warnings**: a *No collider* prop in an opening's clear strip, a prop in an inner doorway's clear strip, a prop where a column stands or may stand, a side of 7–8 cells (it meets the chunk border, where the map decides the edges), and the map's own column and fill notes; the markers' own checks (see *Markers*) |

Doors and windows are not in the panel on purpose: the map puts them where a room meets a zone of another height (decision 1). Edges on the outside of a room that meet another height, or the chunk border, stay as the map makes them, and the map may open one of the room's walls to keep the maze connected. A module prop with a collider on the clear strip of any real opening, yours or one the map opened, is left out (with a console warning).

## In the Scene view

While the designer scene is the active scene (not in Play):

- **Drop** a kit from the palette on the floor: it becomes a prop there, placed by the palette's rules (a green footprint shows where while you drag), and is selected once the preview has rebuilt. A desk-top kit lands on the desk top the mouse is on, not on the floor behind it.
- **Move or turn** a prop with the usual Move and Rotate tools, or its Transform fields: the module follows as you go (X and Z snapped to 0.05 m, only along the axes it moved, so a wall unit keeps its exact distance from the wall; yaw in whole degrees). The preview rebuilds 0.3 s after you let go of the handle or finish typing in a field (never while a handle is held or a field is being typed in: that would destroy the object you are editing) and the prop is selected again. Height is the panel's and scale is not kept: a lifted or scaled prop goes back at the rebuild. One Undo puts the move back in the module and the Scene.
- **Delete** (Edit → Delete, ⌘⌫) removes the selected props from the module, and **Duplicate** (⌘D) adds copies of them half a metre east; both undoable. Deleting or duplicating from the Hierarchy only changes the preview's copies, which the next rebuild puts back; Copy and Paste make plain scene objects, not props.
- Clicking a prop selects the whole kit, not a mesh inside it. A prop selected in the Scene is selected in the window and the other way round, and stays selected across rebuilds.
- The room's outline (orange) and its openings (green, *arch* or *open*) are drawn on the floor, and its markers: a gold disc **K** for a key spot (at its height, with a dotted line down to the floor; its host beside the K), a violet disc **R** as wide as the Relay's body for a Relay entry (its tag beside the R), each with a tick for its yaw. They are only drawn there: edit them in the plan and the Markers section.
- In Prefab Mode and in Play the tools are off.

## How it works

- **`RoomModuleData`** (`FrontRoomsRoomModuleData.cs`, plain C#): the room, its props and its `markers` (`ModuleMarker`: kind, x, z, y, yaw, tag, host; turned with the room like props). **`FrontRoomsRoomModule`**: it as an asset.
- **`RoomModuleStamp.Apply`**: writes a module into a generated chunk. It changes only edges inside the chunk, keeps the map's rule at zone borders, makes the module the chunk's last (fully open) room, places columns, then reopens walls if the room cut the chunk apart (generated walls first). The preview uses it, so the preview is what the game builds.
- **`FrontRoomsMapCache.Place` / `FrontRoomsMapWorld.PlaceModule`**: stamp a module whenever a chunk is generated, again after a revisit shift.
- **`FrontRoomsModulePreview`**: the scene component that rebuilds on every change (in Play only the room's chunk, the walker staying where it stands). It asks the map to tag the module's props (`TagModuleProps`: module, prop index, room origin), keeps the turned data it stamped (`Stamped`, the very object in those tags), raises `Rebuilding` / `Rebuilt`, and converts between module metres and the scene: `ModuleToWorld`, `WorldToModule`, `ModuleToWorldRotation`, `WorldToModuleYaw` (a clockwise quarter turn maps (x, z) to (z, W − x), W the width before the turn, and adds 90° of yaw, as `RoomModuleData.Rotated`).
- **`FrontRoomsLevelDesignerWindow`**: the window. **`FrontRoomsModulePlanView`**: the plan with its prop and marker selections and drag state, one per panel (window and Inspector). **`FrontRoomsModuleGUI`**: the sections both panels draw. **`FrontRoomsModuleEditing`**: every edit to a module (add a kit by the palette's rules, wall snapping, `AgainstWall` which the samples use too, turn, duplicate, remove, reorder; `AddMarker`, `MoveMarker`, `TurnMarker`, `SetMarker`, `RemoveMarkers`, and `TopUnder`, the prop top a key lies on), each undoable and rebuilding the preview. **`FrontRoomsRoomModuleEditor`**: the Inspector.
- **`FrontRoomsDesignerSceneTools`**: the Scene view tools. Moves are seen through `Undo.postprocessModifications`, which Unity calls for the preview's DontSave props as for saved objects; the module is written on the next editor update, because an Undo record made inside that callback is lost (the batch test checks both), and the write is collapsed into the move's undo step. Delete and Duplicate are caught as the Scene view's *SoftDelete* and *Duplicate* commands before Unity acts on the preview's copies.
- **`FrontRoomsLevelDesigner`**: menus, scene, samples, captures, the profile's module list, chance and tier. `-executeMethod FrontRoomsLevelDesigner.ResetSamples -quit` puts the samples back as shipped from the command line (no dialog; the assets and their GUIDs are kept).

Checks: the stamp was checked once outside Unity with a throwaway harness that is not in the project (400 random rooms: borders unchanged, every cell connected, edges, lamps and columns as authored; rotation four times is identity); the 100-seed map check runs in the project. Headless captures: `-executeMethod FrontRoomsLevelDesigner.CaptureBatch -quit` writes `Verification/designer-<module>-eye.png` and `-plan.png`. The P2 tools: `-executeMethod FrontRoomsLevelDesignerTests.RunBatch -quit` writes `Verification/level-designer-tests.json` (138 checks on a temporary module: palette placement against every wall, both faces of an inner wall and beside a doorway, hung kits, desk-top kits on a turned desk; for every turn, round trips and every built prop against the conversions 26 world periods out; Scene moves through Unity's own Undo callback, with no rebuild queued during the move and the rebuild held back while a handle is held or a field typed in; drops (one at a slant onto a desk), duplicate and delete with Undo; the plan's selection following its prop; the generator toggle, chance, tier and fit, and the run tiers a module comes at; the module's checks, with no errors or warnings; markers: for every turn, `ModuleToWorld` and its rotation against `RoomModuleData.Rotated` and the Relay entries the built map registers (with their tags), four single quarter turns back to the start, the marker checks' errors (in a wall, an inner wall, a prop, a custom column, shut in, at the ceiling) and warnings (a second key spot, a raised Relay entry, a key over the eye line, an Auto column spot), a key put on a crate lying on its top and passing, add, move, turn, tag and remove with Undo and the plan's marker selection following its marker; the window letting go of the old module's prop and marker when it switches; a typed field holding the preview back until it lets go of the keyboard; the four samples, in code and as assets, with no errors or warnings; the preview's live rebuild, the one Play uses, run in edit mode: an edit away from the walker rebuilds the room's chunk in the same map with the walker where it stood, a prop or a column put where it stands sends it to the entrance, a new size, seed or profile asks for a full rebuild). The Scene view's own events (the drop, the Delete and Duplicate commands, the drawing) and the plan's mouse and keys need an editor window and are not run there.

## Markers (P4)

Gameplay markers tell the game where two things happen in a room. They turn with the room like props.

| Kind | In the game |
|---|---|
| **Key spot** (K) | When the zone's key falls in this room (the room as the module drew it, in the chunk's own zone), the generator puts the key here instead of on a cell centre: at its x, z, its **height** (on a desk, a cabinet) and **yaw**. **Host**: the kit the key hangs on or lies in (the visual chat's key hosts; *(default)* is the plain key). Only the first key spot of a room is used. A key on the floor keeps a 1 m square clear of the fill; a key on a prop needs none. It is taken from up to 0.9 m away, at any height. |
| **Relay entry** (R) | Where the Relay may appear when it is released or relays: on the floor, facing its yaw. The map collects the entries of the module rooms it built (not in the start area) and the Relay prefers them. **Tag**: what it comes out of (*vent*, *doorway*, or any word), handed to the sound with the arrival; empty for none. The fill (Office kit, pile) keeps a 0.8 m square clear round it. |

**Placing.** **Key spot** or **Relay entry** in the Markers section arms one: click in the plan (a ghost shows where; Shift-click keeps it armed, Esc stops). Or right-click a cell: **Key spot here**, **Relay entry here**. Arming a marker disarms a palette kit and the other way round. Points snap to 0.05 m. A key spot put over a prop lies on its top (its *top* support, else the top of its footprint box), as desk-top items do.

**Editing.** Click a marker in the plan (markers are picked before props): it is ringed in white and its row is highlighted. Drag it (0.05 m, Ctrl 0.5 m; one Undo step, the preview rebuilds when you let go), R turns it 90°, Delete or Backspace removes it, Esc deselects. A prop and a marker are never selected together; markers are not in the Scene selection. The fields: **Kind**, **X**, **Z**, **Yaw** (0 = north); a key spot's **Height** with **On the prop under it** (lays it on the top of the prop there, or on the floor if there is none) and **Host**; a Relay entry's **Tag** with *vent* and *doorway* presets (written as you type; the preview rebuilds once you leave the field). **Turn 90°**, **Remove**. Everything is undoable. In the plan a key spot is a gold disc K, a Relay entry a violet disc R as wide as the Relay's body with its tag under it, each with a tick for its yaw.

**Checks** (in the Checks section with the room's). Errors: a marker in or beyond a wall (a key spot keeps 0.13 m from the cell lines, a Relay entry 0.38 m, its 0.3 m body), in an inner wall, in a custom column; a Relay entry in a prop with a collider on the floor (its body must fit); a key spot inside a prop instead of on it (put it on top: **On the prop under it**); a key spot at or above the ceiling (0.1 m under it); a Relay entry the Relay could not walk out of, or a key spot nobody can get within 0.9 m of (props or inner walls shut it in; checked with the room's walk test). Warnings: a second key spot (only the first is used), a key spot above 1.8 m (over the player's eye line: they may not see it), a Relay entry with a height (it stands on the floor), a marker where an Auto column may stand.

**Samples.** The waiting room's key lies on the side table at the end of the chairs; the store's on the crate at the back; the Office bullpen's Relay comes out of a *vent* low on the east wall; the tall hall's steps in through the north *doorway*.

## In the game (P3)

A module the level profile lists (`Assets/Levels/FrontRoomsLevel0.asset` → *Room modules*; the window's *Used by the generator*) is placed by the generator into the real maze:

- Each carved room that is one open space rolls **Module Chance** (`generation.moduleChance`, 0.3). If it hits, a module with the room's height and theme, whose tier range includes **Module Tier** (`generation.moduleTier`, 0) + the run's tier − 1 (the run starts at tier 1 and rises to the profile's last tier), and that fits the room in some allowed quarter turn, is chosen by **weight** (shared between its fitting turns).
- It is stamped at a hashed spot in the room, off the chunk border when the room leaves room for that, so the walls you drew stay yours. A module with **Auto** columns lands on the 6 m grid phase its turn has in the preview, so the columns stand where the preview shows them for that turn (with an odd side, odd turns move them to the other corners; the checks warn when a prop stands where any allowed turn puts one). Auto columns never stand where an inner wall or doorway meets. A module listed twice counts once; weights above 1000 are capped. A prop with a collider on the clear strip of a real opening is left out with a console warning: your own openings (the checks make that an error) and the edges the map decides (zone and chunk borders, a wall reopened to keep the maze connected). So is a prop in a column (the checks warn), and a desk-top item whose desk was left out. With **Fill** Office or Pile, the kit keeps out of your inner walls and inner doorways.
- The same seed always gives the same modules; a revisit shift may bring a different one. The 100-seed check runs with the library and reports how many it placed (it fails if the library is in use and none was placed); the debug map (**FrontRooms → Map → Debug map**) outlines them in orange.
- The four samples are in the library from the start. The preview turns the generator's modules off around the room you edit, so only yours is there.
